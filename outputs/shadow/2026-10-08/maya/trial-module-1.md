Spoken:
Welcome. This first module is short and it has one job. By the end, you will know what the system does for your business, and just as importantly, what it does not do.

Let's start with what it is. The system is a customer response agent. It helps with routine questions, the kind where the answer already sits in a document your business has approved. Those questions can arrive by email, by phone, or through the portal. However a question arrives, the system follows the same path, and there are five steps.

Step one. A question arrives, by email, phone or portal.

Step two. The system checks the question against the documents your business provided and approved. Think of lease or policy terms, portal steps, and office hours. These approved documents are its only source. It does not guess, and it does not go looking anywhere else.

Step three. If the question can be answered from those approved documents, the response goes out.

Step four. If the question is outside those documents, or it is urgent, the system does not try to answer it. It sends the question to a person. That means your named human operator and the office.

Step five. A person reviews what the system could not answer. That review is part of the design, not an extra. Your named human operator is responsible for it.

Now let's be clear about what the system does not do. It does not answer from anything other than your approved documents. It does not make judgment calls. It does not handle urgent questions; those go to a person. It does not replace your named human operator or the office.

Some things you might expect are also outside this system. It does not do inspections. It does not do scheduling. It does not do work-order handoff. It does not do document intake. It does not connect to other platforms or systems. And it does not do open-request tracking. If something is not covered in this training library, treat it as not part of your system, and ask us before assuming.

A word on why it is built this way. Many questions your office answers repeat again and again. The system takes on that repetitive work, so people can spend their time on unusual situations, urgent matters and anything that needs judgment. People stay in charge of those, and of reviewing what the system could not answer.

Here is a simple picture. Someone asks what time the office opens. That answer is in your approved documents, so the response goes out. Now someone asks about a situation your documents do not cover, or the message sounds urgent. The system does not improvise. It hands the question to a person. You will see both paths in the next modules, so you do not need to memorize anything today.

Here are three things to remember. One, the system answers only from documents your business approved. Two, anything outside those documents, or urgent, goes to a person. Three, it does not do inspections, scheduling, work-order handoff, document intake, connecting to other systems, or open-request tracking.
